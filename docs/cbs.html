Parameter settings

The knobs behind every number on this page, in the two groups src/provenance.py partitions them into, which is also why they are read from different places (a third column regroups the cadence-coupled ones, which are pulled out of these two rather than repeated). A curator knob acts upstream of the journal, so the honest value is the one stamped in data/cbs_v12/provenance.json when the curation ran: investor_profile.forward.md is a live file, and a knob edited there since would describe a curation that could not have produced this journal. An optimizer knob acts at replay time, and this build just replayed the journal under the profile as it stands now, so that is where those come from. Changing a curator knob means buying the curation again, while changing an optimizer knob costs a rebuild. The caps bound what this page can show: max_events and max_new_events limit how many events a scan may open, and max_watchlist limits how many the optimizer may fund, so read them alongside Breadth over time and Watchlist composition.

4. Event score vs what the event earned

Does a better-ranked event actually make more money? Every funded event goes into a RANK BUCKET — its rank is the median of its own places over the scans it was ranked, since one call re-ranks every live event at every scan — and each bucket is drawn at the median of its events' realized P&L divided by the number of months the optimizer actually funded them. Left is better: rank 1 is the best event of its scan. Scans hold 25 to 50 events, median 33, so a raw rank is comparable across them without normalising. Per event and not per event-month, so one long-held name counts once instead of dominating its bucket; and a median rather than a mean, because a single event's P&L is heavy-tailed enough to drag any average it lands in. The bars are sd/√n over the events in the bin — the standard deviation of the mean. (The median's own asymptotic error is about 1.25× that, so these bars are the tighter of the two, not the looser.) P&L is split between events that share a ticker, by the same rule the reports use. Buckets hold EQUAL NUMBERS of events, not equal score widths, so the low bucket spans a wider score range than the tight middle ones — with 93 events that is 18-21 apiece. An event counts as funded for a period only if it is still live THROUGH it: a weight set at an anchor is held across the month that follows, so an event ending ON that anchor holds nothing during the period being measured. Read it against non-negotiable #6: this is one curation, and the bars say how little one curation can settle.
