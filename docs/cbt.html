2. Watchlist composition over time

One row per ticker. The pale bar is the span the curator kept it WATCHLISTED — it held the thesis; the solid bar is the span the optimizer actually FUNDED it. 3. Cumulative $ gain per holding

The 10 best and 5 worst funded names, with every other name rolled into one grey bar. A result resting on one or two names is a different thing from the same return spread across many — and the difference is not visible in the equity curve above. Click any named bar for that ticker’s price history, with ▲/▼ marking the moments the optimizer funded and unfunded it.

4. Perfect-foresight ceiling per ticker

For each name, the best single buy→sell that was available INSIDE the span the curator held it watchlisted (pale), against what simply holding it start-to-finish returned (solid). No credit for names it never found. A LOW ceiling means the pick itself was a dud; a HIGH ceiling with a weak hold means the pick was fine and the timing was not. Hindsight upper bound — not attainable. Click any bar to open that ticker’s price history, with the watchlisted span shaded and the funded span drawn heavier.

5. Cumulative $ gain per beat

The same dollars as the panel above, rolled up to the BEAT that surfaced each ticker’s evidence — i.e. which part of the firehose paid. A beat that costs money is a retrieval-vocabulary problem, not a curator one.

6. Cumulative $ gain per event

The same gains grouped by the EVENT that motivated them. PWR groups this by wave bucket; GHR's unit of thesis is the event, so this is its analogue. It answers whether the curator's ideas paid, independently of which vehicle expressed them.

7. Portfolio $ by event over time

How the book was distributed across events as the year ran. Wide bands that persist mean concentrated conviction; a churn of thin bands means the optimizer kept rotating.

8. Allocation over time

Dollars held per ticker, stacked — the top edge is the portfolio value. The always_include anchors (SPY, GLD, BIL) sit outside the watchlist cap and are where idle capital parks; gaps are uninvested cash.

9. Curator funnel

Everything the curator touched, from the articles it read down to the picks it logged. The direct analogue of the firehose funnel — but here the interesting collapse is at the top: a whole week of articles yields a handful of candidates. Log x-axis.

10. Breadth over time
