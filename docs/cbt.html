4. Cumulative $ gain per holding

The 16 best and 8 worst funded names, with every other name rolled into one grey bar. A result resting on one or two names is a different thing from the same return spread across many — and the difference is not visible in the equity curve above. Click any named bar for that ticker’s price history, with ▲/▼ marking the moments the optimizer funded and unfunded it.

5. Cumulative $ gain per beat

The same dollars as the panel above, rolled up to the BEAT that surfaced each ticker’s evidence — i.e. which part of the firehose paid. A beat that costs money is a retrieval-vocabulary problem, not a curator one.

6. Gain per article read, by beat

The same dollars divided by how many of that beat's articles actually REACHED THE SCOUT — i.e. survived the discovery gate. That is the cost that binds: the scout is 91% of the LLM bill and reads only gate-passed articles. The corpus count and the pass rate are in the hover. They diverge sharply — the momentum beat passes at 31% against 3-8% elsewhere, because its atoms ARE the gate's vocabulary, so it is 17% of the corpus but 35% of everything the scout reads. Beats that retrieved articles but never produced a funded position sit at zero: they are pure cost. Read it against panel 5 — a tall bar there with a short bar here is a beat carried by volume rather than by quality.

7. Cumulative $ gain per event

The same gains grouped by the EVENT that motivated them. PWR groups this by wave bucket; GHR's unit of thesis is the event, so this is its analogue. It answers whether the curator's ideas paid, independently of which vehicle expressed them.

8. Portfolio value by event over time

How the book was distributed across events as the year ran. Wide bands that persist mean concentrated conviction; a churn of thin bands means the optimizer kept rotating.

9. Allocation over time

Dollars held per ticker, stacked — the top edge is the portfolio value. The always_include anchors (SPY, GLD, BIL) sit outside the watchlist cap and are where idle capital parks. Uninvested cash is drawn as its own dark band rather than left as a gap, so an empty stretch reads as a decision, not as missing data.

10. Thesis concentration

How much of the FUNDED book its single largest EVENT held, day by day. concentration_cap limits any one TICKER, but nothing limits a THESIS: an event naming four vehicles can take the whole book while every position looks disciplined under the cap. The dashed line is the per-ticker cap for scale — the gap between it and the solid line is the exposure the cap does not see. Read the time above 80%: those are days the portfolio was one bet wearing several tickers, so a single thesis being wrong takes the whole book with it.

11. Curator funnel

Everything the curator touched, from the articles it read down to the picks it logged. The direct analogue of the firehose funnel — but here the interesting collapse is at the top: a whole week of articles yields a handful of candidates. Log x-axis.

12. Breadth over time

What used to sit between the solid and dashed lines was inventory the optimizer was never going to fund.

Read it beside Breadth over time: breadth without precision is noise, precision without breadth is luck.
